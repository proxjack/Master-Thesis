\chapter{Theoretical Background}







This chapter introduces the physical and circuit-level concepts required to motivate the design choices made in the following chapters.
In this chapter, starting from Faraday's law, an electrical
model of the variable-reluctance energy harvester (VREH) is
derived. The model is then used to discuss the maximum
power transfer theorem and the role of impedance matching

\section{Electrical Model of the Variable-Reluctance Harvester}


\subsection{Faraday's law}

The Faraday's law is the starting point for any electromagnetic transducer, which relates the electromotive force $V_c(t)$ (EMF) induced in a closed loop wire to the time rate of change of the magnetic flux $\Phi(t)$ linked to the loop. 

\begin{equation}
    V_c(t) = -\frac{\dd \Phi_i(t)}{\dd t}
    \label{eq:faraday}
\end{equation}

For a coil made by $N_t$ turns sharing the same flux, the total linked flux is $\Lambda(t)=N\,\Phi_i(t)$ and the induced EMF becomes:

\begin{equation}
    V_c(t) = -\frac{\dd \Lambda(t)}{\dd t} = -N_t\frac{\dd \Phi_i(t)}{\dd t}.
    \label{eq:faraday_N}
\end{equation}

In case of a coil consisting of $N_t$ turns mechanically spaced one respect to each other leading to different linked flux, the induce voltage can be described by

\begin{equation}
V_c(t) = -\frac{d}{dt} \cdot \sum_{n=i}^{N_t} \Phi_i(t)
\label{eq:faraday_dis}
\end{equation}


where $\Phi_i$ is the flux linkage associated to a single turn of the coil\cite{xuSurveyVariableReluctance2018a}. 

\subsection{Variable Reluctance Principle}

According to Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction, a VREH converts kinetic energy to electrical energy.
An electromotive force (EMF) is induced across the pickup
coil that couples to the time-varying magnetic flux caused
by the rotating toothed wheel, and the variation in reluctance
of the system \cite{xuSurveyVariableReluctance2018a}.

    \label{fig:VREH_basic_struct}
\end{figure}




















The magnetic flux is produced by a permanent magnet whose
field follows a fixed path through a ferromagnetic stator
and a moving rotor.
The rotor introduces a periodic variation of the magnetic
reluctance $\mathcal{R}(\theta)$ of the magnetic circuit,
where $\theta$ denotes the mechanical angle of rotation.
If the magnetomotive force of the permanent magnet is
denoted $\mathcal{F}_m$ and is assumed constant, the flux
through the coil is governed by Hopkinson's law
\cite{fitzgeraldElectricMachinery2003}:
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_B(\theta)
    \;=\;
    \frac{\mathcal{F}_m}{\mathcal{R}(\theta)}.
    \label{eq:hopkinson}
\end{equation}
For a rotor turning at constant angular velocity
$\omega = \dd\theta/\dd t$, the chain rule applied to
\eqref{eq:faraday-N} together with \eqref{eq:hopkinson}
yields
\begin{equation}
    \varepsilon(t)
    \;=\;
    -N\,\frac{\dd \Phi_B}{\dd \theta}\,\frac{\dd \theta}{\dd t}
    \;=\;
    N\,\mathcal{F}_m\,
    \frac{1}{\mathcal{R}^2(\theta)}\,
    \frac{\dd \mathcal{R}(\theta)}{\dd \theta}\,\omega.
    \label{eq:emf-reluctance}
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:emf-reluctance} shows the two features
that characterise variable-reluctance harvesters. First,
the open-circuit voltage scales linearly with the angular
velocity $\omega$, hence with the rotational speed of the
host system. Second, the waveform is not sinusoidal in
general: its shape is dictated by the geometric profile
$\mathcal{R}(\theta)$ of the magnetic
circuit \cite{xuThreephaseVariableReluctance2022}.
 
Under the working assumption that the reluctance variation
is well approximated by its fundamental Fourier component
(an assumption justified by a posteriori observation of the
measured waveforms in
\cite{wuMultiPhaseVariableReluctance2024}), the induced EMF
of a single phase reduces to a sinusoid of amplitude
proportional to $\omega$:
\begin{equation}
    \varepsilon(t) \;=\; E_0(\omega)\,\sin(\omega t),
    \qquad
    E_0(\omega) \;\propto\; \omega.
    \label{eq:emf-sinusoidal}
\end{equation}
In the six-phase machine considered in this work, the
stator coils are spatially distributed so that the six
generated EMFs are mutually phase-shifted by
$\Delta\varphi = 2\pi/6 = \pi/3\,\text{rad}$. The EMF of
the $k$-th phase, $k\in\{1,\dots,6\}$, is therefore
\begin{equation}
    \varepsilon_k(t)
    \;=\;
    E_0(\omega)\,
    \sin\!\left(\omega t - (k-1)\,\frac{\pi}{3}\right).
    \label{eq:emf-sixphase}
\end{equation}
